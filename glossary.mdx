## A

### Atom Serial Number
The unique identifier for each atom in a PDB file, shown in columns 7-11. LNKD uses these numbers in output files to specify which atoms should bond together.

**Example**: In output `59 1054`, atoms with serial numbers 59 and 1054 will form a bond.

### Azide
A functional group containing three nitrogen atoms (N₃), used in click chemistry surface cross-linking. The terminal nitrogen forms bonds with alkyne carbons to create triazole rings.

---

## B

### Bond Potential
A scoring function that combines geometric favorability (distance) with topological preference (isolatedness) to rank candidate atom pairs. Higher potential = more likely to form a bond.

**Formula**: See [Bond Potential Function](/science/algorithm/bond-potential) for mathematical details.

---

## C

### Chain
A single polymer molecule in your system. In MINP context, can refer to surfactants, cross-linkers (like DVB), or template molecules. LNKD enforces that cross-links only form *between* chains, not within a single chain.

### Click Chemistry
Azide-alkyne cycloaddition reaction used for surface cross-linking in MINPs. Forms triazole rings with TWO N-C bonds per reaction. Known for high efficiency and biocompatibility.

**Related**: [Click Chemistry Details](/science/chemistry/click-chemistry)

### Core Cross-Linking
The second stage of MINP synthesis, polymerizing the interior using radical chemistry (C-C bond formation). Uses smaller query radius (typically 10 Å) than surface cross-linking.

**Contrast**: [Surface Cross-Linking](#surface-cross-linking)

### Coverage
Percentage of reactive atoms that successfully pair and form bonds. LNKD typically achieves 88-95% coverage.

**Formula**: Coverage = (2 × pairs) / total_reactive_atoms × 100%

---

## D

### DVB (Divinylbenzene)
A common cross-linking monomer with two vinyl groups (C=C double bonds). Used in core cross-linking for radical polymerization. Comes in ortho (DVO) and para (DVP) isomers.

---

## G

### Greedy Algorithm
LNKD's selection strategy: iteratively choose the highest-probability pair, accept it, update affected pairs, and repeat. Not globally optimal but fast (O(m log n)) and achieves high coverage.

**Related**: [Greedy Selection Strategy](/science/algorithm/algorithm-concepts#greedy-selection-strategy)

---

## H

### Hydrogen Saturation
Adding hydrogen atoms to unpaired radicals (vinyl carbons that didn't find bonding partners) to prevent unrealistic chemistry in MD simulations. Required post-processing step for core cross-linking.

**Why needed**: Radicals with unpaired electrons are highly reactive and can cause MD simulation instability.

---

## I

### Isolatedness
A topological measure of how many external bonds a chain currently has. Higher isolatedness = fewer existing bonds. LNKD's bond potential includes an isolatedness term to promote uniform cross-linking.

**Formula**: Isolatedness = 8 - (current connectivity)

### Isolation Weight (W)
User parameter (0.0-1.0) controlling the strength of preference for bonding to isolated chains. Typical value: 0.03 (slight preference).

**Effect**:
- W = 0: Pure distance-based selection
- W = 0.03: Balanced (recommended)
- W > 0.1: Strong preference, may reduce coverage

---

## K

### KD-Tree
K-dimensional tree - a spatial data structure that partitions 3D space for efficient neighbor queries. LNKD's core innovation for achieving O(n log n) complexity.

**Key advantage**: Find nearby atoms in O(log n) time instead of O(n) linear search.

**Related**: [KD-Trees Explained](/science/algorithm/algorithm-concepts#kd-trees)

---

## M

### Max Heap
A binary tree data structure where parent nodes have higher values than children. LNKD uses a max heap to efficiently access the highest-probability pair at each iteration.

**Complexity**: O(1) to peek max, O(log n) to pop and reheapify.

### MINP (Molecularly Imprinted Nanoparticle)
Synthetic polymer nanoparticles (5-50 nm) with designed binding sites that mimic biological recognition. Created by polymerizing around a template molecule, then removing the template.

**Applications**: Sensors, drug delivery, separation, artificial antibodies.

**Related**: [MIP Background](/science/chemistry/mip-background)

---

## O

### O(n log n) Complexity
Computational complexity notation meaning that doubling the number of atoms (n) increases runtime by approximately 2.1× (not 4× as with O(n²) methods).

**Pronunciation**: "Big-O of n log n"

**Practical meaning**: LNKD scales efficiently to large polymer systems.

---

## P

### Polymer Chain
See [Chain](#chain)

---

## Q

### Query Radius (QR)
Maximum distance (in angstroms) LNKD searches for potential bonding partners. Atoms farther apart than the query radius won't be considered.

**Typical values**:
- Core: 10 Å (short-range radical coupling)
- Surface: 15 Å (longer-range click chemistry)

**Trade-off**: Larger radius captures more pairs but increases computation time.

---

## R

### Radical
An atom with an unpaired electron. In LNKD context, refers to vinyl carbons that didn't find bonding partners during core cross-linking prediction. Listed in `radical_output.txt`.

**Post-processing**: Requires hydrogen saturation.

### Radical Polymerization
Chemical reaction where vinyl groups (C=C) form C-C single bonds via radical mechanism. Used for core cross-linking in MINPs.

**Related**: [Radical Polymerization Details](/science/chemistry/radical-polymerization)

### Reactive Atoms
Atoms in your polymer system that can participate in cross-linking reactions. You specify these atoms by creating reactive atom definition files.

**Examples**:
- Vinyl carbons (CU, CV) for radical polymerization
- Azide nitrogens (N01-N03) for click chemistry
- Alkyne carbons (C14, C16) for click chemistry

---

## S

### Surface Cross-Linking
The first stage of MINP synthesis, cross-linking the outer shell using click chemistry (azide-alkyne cycloaddition). Uses larger query radius (typically 15 Å) than core cross-linking.

**Purpose**: Creates rigid shell to confine the core during subsequent polymerization.

---

## T

### Triazole
A 5-membered heterocyclic ring containing three nitrogen atoms and two carbons, formed by click chemistry. Each click reaction creates TWO bonds (two N-C bonds forming the ring).

**Why two bonds?** The triazole ring closes by forming bonds between the azide nitrogen and BOTH alkyne carbons.

---

## V

### Vinyl Carbon
A carbon atom in a C=C double bond (alkene). In radical polymerization, vinyl carbons form new C-C single bonds with other vinyl carbons. DVB has two vinyl groups, providing four reactive vinyl carbons per molecule.

**Notation**: Often labeled CU (unsubstituted) and CV (vinyl position) in PDB files.
