## Overview

LNKD builds on decades of research in molecular imprinting, spatial algorithms, and polymer network modeling. This page provides context and pointers to related work.

---

## Molecular Imprinting

### Foundational Work

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="MIP Discovery">
    **Original molecular imprinting**:

    Wulff, G., & Sarhan, A. (1972). "Über die Anwendung von enzymanalog gebauten Polymeren zur Racemattrennung." *Angewandte Chemie*, 84(8), 364.

    **Key contribution**: First demonstration of template-directed polymer synthesis for selective molecular recognition

    **Limitation**: Bulk polymerization → heterogeneous binding sites

    **LNKD connection**: LNKD enables computational prediction of cross-linking patterns, improving binding site uniformity
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Nanoparticle MIPs">
    Poma, A., Guerreiro, A., Whitcombe, M. J., Piletska, E. V., Turner, A. P., & Piletsky, S. A. (2013). "Solid-phase synthesis of molecularly imprinted polymer nanoparticles with a reusable template–'plastic antibodies'." *Advanced Functional Materials*, 23(22), 2821-2827.

    **Key contribution**: Solid-phase synthesis protocol for uniform MINPs with controlled size

    **Innovation**: Template immobilized on glass beads → monodisperse nanoparticles

    **LNKD application**: LNKD predicts cross-linking for solid-phase MINP construction
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Computational MIP Design">
    Chianella, I., Lotierzo, M., Piletsky, S. A., Tothill, I. E., Chen, B., Karim, K., & Turner, A. P. (2002). "Rational design of a polymer specific for microcystin-LR using a computational approach." *Analytical Chemistry*, 74(6), 1288-1293.

    **Key contribution**: First use of molecular modeling for MIP monomer selection

    **Method**: Molecular dynamics + binding energy calculations

    **LNKD advancement**: Explicit cross-linking prediction (vs. monomer selection only)
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Spatial Algorithms

### KD-Trees

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Original Algorithm">
    **Bentley, J. L. (1975)**. "Multidimensional binary search trees used for associative searching." *Communications of the ACM*, 18(9), 509-517.

    **Contribution**: Introduced KD-trees for multi-dimensional search

    **Complexity**:
    - Construction: O(n log n)
    - Nearest neighbor: O(log n) average, O(n) worst-case
    - Range query: O(n^(1-1/k) + m) where m = matches

    **LNKD usage**: Radius queries for reactive atom neighbors
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Implementations">
    **SciPy KDTree** (used by LNKD):

    ```python
    from scipy.spatial import KDTree

    tree = KDTree(coords)
    indices, distances = tree.query_radius(
        coords, r=15.0, return_distance=True
    )
    ```

    **Alternatives**:
    - `sklearn.neighbors.KDTree`: Scikit-learn implementation
    - `pyKDTree`: Pure Python (slower)
    - `nanoflann`: C++ library (fastest)

    **LNKD choice**: SciPy for balance of speed and ease of use
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Alternative Data Structures">
    **Ball trees**:
    - Better for high dimensions (d > 20)
    - Similar O(log n) queries
    - Not needed for 3D molecular coordinates

    **Octrees**:
    - Spatial partitioning into octants
    - Good for graphics, physics simulations
    - Less efficient than KD-trees for nearest neighbor

    **Uniform grids**:
    - Fast construction O(n)
    - O(1) neighbor lookup with optimal cell size
    - Poor for non-uniform distributions (micelles!)
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Polymer Network Modeling

### Cross-linking Simulation Methods

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Monte Carlo Cross-linking" icon="dice">
    **Method**: Stochastic bond formation based on distance cutoffs

    **Example**: Gissinger, J. R., et al. (2017). "Modeling chemical reactions in classical molecular dynamics simulations." *Polymer*, 128, 211-217.

    **Limitation**: No prioritization, ignores chain saturation

    **LNKD improvement**: Probabilistic scoring with isolatedness
  </Card>

  <Card title="Reactive MD" icon="atom">
    **Method**: ReaxFF force field with explicit bond breaking/forming

    **Example**: van Duin, A. C., et al. (2001). "ReaxFF: A reactive force field for hydrocarbons." *J. Phys. Chem. A*, 105(41), 9396-9409.

    **Advantage**: True chemical reactivity

    **Limitation**: Computationally expensive (100x slower than classical MD)

    **LNKD use case**: Prediction step guides where to apply reactive MD
  </Card>

  <Card title="Constraint-Based" icon="link">
    **Method**: Distance restraints applied in MD (PLUMED, AMBER)

    **Example**: Tribello, G. A., et al. (2014). "PLUMED 2: New feathers for an old bird." *Comp. Phys. Comm.*, 185(2), 604-613.

    **LNKD integration**: Generates PLUMED input from predictions
  </Card>

  <Card title="Graph-Based" icon="project-diagram">
    **Method**: Network topology analysis of cross-linked polymers

    **Example**: Hagita, K., et al. (2016). "Coarse-grained molecular dynamics simulation of filled polymer nanocomposites." *Macromolecules*, 49(5), 1972-1983.

    **LNKD output**: Pair lists convertible to NetworkX graphs
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Nanoparticle Design

### Computational Screening

<Steps>
  <Step title="Protein Design Inspiration">
    **Rosetta** (Leaver-Fay et al., 2011):
    - Monte Carlo sampling of protein conformations
    - Energy-based scoring functions
    - High-throughput mutant screening

    **LNKD parallel**: Greedy pair selection analogous to rotamer packing
  </Step>

  <Step title="Materials Informatics">
    **Machine learning for materials** (Butler et al., 2018):
    - Train on experimental data (structure → properties)
    - Screen thousands of candidates
    - Guide synthesis priorities

    **LNKD opportunity**: Use predictions as features for ML models (cross-link density → binding affinity)
  </Step>

  <Step title="Docking and Virtual Screening">
    **AutoDock Vina** (Trott & Olson, 2010):
    - Predict protein-ligand binding poses
    - Score by energy function
    - Screen compound libraries

    **MINP analog**: LNKD predicts structure → MD → binding prediction
  </Step>
</Steps>

    ```tcl
        lassign $line atom1 atom2
        draw line [atomselect top "serial $atom1"] [atomselect top "serial $atom2"] width 3
    }
    ```

    **PyMOL**
    - Publication-quality figures
    - Cross-link visualization scripts
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

### Open Problems

<Warning>
  **Challenges for the community**:
  1. **Solvent effects**: LNKD currently ignores explicit solvent during prediction (relies on post-MD validation)
  2. **Stereochemistry**: No explicit handling of chiral centers in cross-linking reactions
  3. **Template flexibility**: Assumes rigid template during predictions (conformational changes not modeled)
  4. **Scalability**: Systems >50,000 atoms still challenging (KD-tree limitations)
</Warning>
