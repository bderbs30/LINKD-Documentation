## The Bond Potential Equation

LNKD scores each candidate pair using a **bond potential** function that combines geometric favorability (distance) with topological preference (isolatedness).

### Full Equation

$$P = \frac{\exp\left(-\frac{(d - d_{eq})^2}{2\sigma^2}\right) + w \cdot I}{1 + w \cdot C_{max}}$$

**Where**:
- $P$ = Bond potential (probability score)
- $d$ = Distance between atoms (Å)
- $d_{eq}$ = Equilibrium bond distance (3.0 Å)
- $\sigma^2$ = Distance variance (6.25)
- $w$ = Isolation weight (user parameter, typically 0.03)
- $I$ = Isolatedness score (topological measure)
- $C_{max}$ = Maximum connectivity (8)

---

## Distance Term (Gaussian Component)

The first term is a **Gaussian function** centered at the equilibrium bond distance.

### Mathematical Form

$$\text{Distance Score} = \exp\left(-\frac{(d - d_{eq})^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$$

### Parameters

| Parameter | Value | Meaning |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| $d_{eq}$ | 3.0 Å | Ideal C-C bond distance for vinyl radicals |
| $\sigma^2$ | 6.25 | Variance (controls width of Gaussian) |
| $\sigma$ | 2.5 Å | Standard deviation |

### Distance Sensitivity

The Gaussian falls off as atoms deviate from ideal bonding distance:

| Distance (Å) | Gaussian Value | Percentage | Interpretation |
|--------------|----------------|------------|----------------|
| 3.0 | 1.000 | 100% | Perfect |
| 3.5 | 0.980 | 98% | Excellent |
| 4.0 | 0.923 | 92% | Very good |
| 4.5 | 0.819 | 82% | Good |
| 5.0 | 0.726 | 73% | Acceptable |
| 6.0 | 0.527 | 53% | Marginal |
| 7.0 | 0.333 | 33% | Poor |
| 10.0 | 0.074 | 7% | Very unlikely |

<Tip>
  Query radius of 10-15 Å ensures capture of all reasonable candidates while excluding very low probability pairs.
</Tip>

<Accordion title="Why Gaussian Instead of Hard Cutoff?">
  **Gaussian advantages**:
  1. **Smooth falloff**: Avoids artificial discontinuity at cutoff
  2. **Probabilistic interpretation**: Naturally represents uncertainty in bond distance
  3. **Physical realism**: Bond formation probability decreases continuously with distance
  4. **Numerical stability**: No sudden jumps in scoring function

  **Hard cutoff problems**:
  - Pair at 4.99 Å gets full score, pair at 5.01 Å gets zero
  - Arbitrary threshold selection
  - Poor representation of chemical reality
</Accordion>

## Isolatedness Term (Topological Component)

The second term promotes bonding to **isolated chains** - chains with fewer existing external bonds.

### Calculation

For a candidate pair bonding chain A to chain B:

$$I = I_A + I_B$$

Where:
$$I_{\text{chain}} = C_{max} - \text{current\_connectivity}$$

**Current connectivity** = number of other chains this chain is already bonded to.

### Example Calculation

**Chain A**:
- Already bonded to chains C, D, E
- Connectivity = 3
- $I_A = 8 - 3 = 5$

**Chain B**:
- No external bonds yet
- Connectivity = 0
- $I_B = 8 - 0 = 8$

**Total**: $I = 5 + 8 = 13$

### Isolatedness Scale

| Chain Connectivity | Isolatedness | Interpretation |
|--------------------|--------------|----------------|
| 0 (virgin chain) | 8 | Maximum bonus |
| 2 | 6 | High bonus |
| 4 | 4 | Moderate bonus |
| 6 | 2 | Low bonus |
| 8 (saturated) | 0 | No bonus |

<Warning>
  Cmax = 8 is a design choice that prevents excessive cross-linking density while allowing sufficient network connectivity.
</Warning>

---

## Normalization

The denominator normalizes the bond potential to prevent unbounded growth:

$$\text{Normalizer} = 1 + w \cdot C_{max}$$

With $w = 0.03$ and $C_{max} = 8$:
$$\text{Normalizer} = 1 + 0.03 \times 8 = 1.24$$

This ensures:
- Maximum possible $P \approx 0.99$ (when $d = d_{eq}$ and both chains fully isolated)
- Minimum $P = 0$ (when constraints violated)

## Parameter Tuning

Users control bond potential through two parameters:

### Query Radius (QR)

**Effect**: Determines which pairs are considered

- **Small (5-8 Å)**: Only very close atoms
  - Faster execution
  - May miss valid pairs
  - Risk: Low coverage

- **Medium (10-12 Å)**: Recommended default
  - Balances speed and coverage
  - Captures most chemically reasonable pairs

- **Large (15-20 Å)**: Permissive search
  - Slower execution
  - May include unlikely long-range pairs
  - Risk: Clustering

<Tip>
  Start with QR = 10 Å for core, 15 Å for surface. Adjust based on coverage.
</Tip>

### Isolation Weight (w)

**Effect**: Controls strength of isolatedness preference

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="w = 0.00">
    **Pure distance-based selection**

    Bond potential:
    $$P = \exp\left(-\frac{(d - d_{eq})^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$$

    **Behavior**:
    - Pairs selected solely by geometric favorability
    - Risk of clustering (multiple bonds to same chain)
    - May leave some chains isolated
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="w = 0.03 (Recommended)">
    **Balanced selection**

    Isolatedness contributes ~20-30% to total score for virgin chains.

    **Behavior**:
    - Slight preference for isolated chains
    - Achieves 88-95% coverage
    - Uniform cross-linking distribution
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="w = 0.10">
    **Strong isolatedness preference**

    Isolatedness dominates for low-connectivity chains.

    **Behavior**:
    - Aggressively avoids re-bonding to same chains
    - May reject geometrically favorable pairs
    - Risk: Lower overall coverage
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Worked Example

Consider a candidate pair:

**Atoms**:
- Atom 1 on Chain A (already bonded to chains C, D)
- Atom 2 on Chain B (no external bonds)
- Distance: 3.5 Å

**Parameters**:
- $w = 0.03$
- $d_{eq} = 3.0$ Å
- $\sigma^2 = 6.25$
- $C_{max} = 8$

### Step 1: Calculate Distance Term

$$\exp\left(-\frac{(3.5 - 3.0)^2}{2 \times 6.25}\right) = \exp\left(-\frac{0.25}{12.5}\right) = \exp(-0.02) = 0.980$$

### Step 2: Calculate Isolatedness

- Chain A connectivity: 2 → $I_A = 8 - 2 = 6$
- Chain B connectivity: 0 → $I_B = 8 - 0 = 8$
- Total: $I = 6 + 8 = 14$

### Step 3: Calculate Isolatedness Contribution

$$w \cdot I = 0.03 \times 14 = 0.42$$

### Step 4: Calculate Normalizer

$$1 + w \cdot C_{max} = 1 + 0.03 \times 8 = 1.24$$

### Step 5: Final Bond Potential

$$P = \frac{0.980 + 0.42}{1.24} = \frac{1.40}{1.24} = 1.13$$

Wait, that's > 1! This is normalized to 0.99 in practice (clipped to valid probability range).

<Accordion title="Why can calculated P exceed 1?">
  The formula can theoretically produce $P > 1$ when:
  - Distance is near-ideal ($d \approx d_{eq}$)
  - Both chains are highly isolated (high $I$)

  **In practice**: LNKD implementation clips $P$ to maximum 0.99 to maintain valid probability interpretation.

  This doesn't affect algorithm behavior since pairs are ranked by $P$, and relative ordering is preserved.
</Accordion>

How much does each parameter affect the final score?

## Code Implementation

From `pair_prediction/tools/predict_bonds.py:103-117`:

```python
def bond_potential(self, atom_dist, dist_equilibrium, dist_variance,
                   iso_weight, isolatedness, Cmax):
    """
    Calculate bond potential for a pair.

    Args:
        atom_dist: Distance between atoms (Å)
        dist_equilibrium: Ideal bond distance (Å)
        dist_variance: Variance in distance distribution
        iso_weight: Weight for isolatedness term
        isolatedness: 8 - current_connectivity
        Cmax: Maximum connectivity (8)

    Returns:
        Bond potential (0 to ~1)
    """
    return (
        (np.exp(-((atom_dist - dist_equilibrium) ** 2) / (2 * dist_variance))
         + (iso_weight * isolatedness))
        / (1 + iso_weight * Cmax)
    )
```

<Accordion title="Why NumPy exp instead of math.exp?">
  **Vectorization**: LNKD calculates bond potential for many pairs simultaneously.

  NumPy's `np.exp()` is vectorized - it can operate on entire arrays at once, providing significant performance advantages over looping with `math.exp()`.

  **Key benefit**: Vectorized operations leverage optimized BLAS/LAPACK libraries and avoid Python loop overhead.
</Accordion>
