## What is Click Chemistry?

**Click chemistry** is a set of reactions that are:
- High yielding (>95%)
- Regioselective (single product)
- Thermodynamically favorable
- Insensitive to oxygen and water
- Simple to perform

**Most famous example**: Azide-alkyne cycloaddition (AAC)

---

## Historical Context

<Steps>
  <Step title="2001: Sharpless Defines Click Chemistry">
    **K. Barry Sharpless** introduces click chemistry philosophy:
    - "Spring-loaded" reactions (high thermodynamic driving force)
    - Modular and wide in scope
    - Minimal purification needed
    - Physiological conditions compatible

    **Goal**: Simplify chemical synthesis, especially for drug discovery
  </Step>

  <Step title="2002: Meldal and Sharpless Independently Develop CuAAC">
    **Copper(I)-catalyzed azide-alkyne cycloaddition (CuAAC)**:
    - Cu(I) catalyst accelerates reaction 10⁷× vs. uncatalyzed
    - Room temperature, aqueous conditions
    - Regioselective for 1,4-disubstituted triazole
    - **"The cream of the crop of click chemistry"** - Sharpless

    **Nobel Prize**: Sharpless shares 2022 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for click chemistry
  </Step>

  <Step title="2004: Bertozzi Develops Copper-Free Click">
    **Strain-promoted azide-alkyne cycloaddition (SPAAC)**:
    - Uses strained cyclooctynes instead of terminal alkynes
    - No copper catalyst required (bioorthogonal)
    - Slower than CuAAC but compatible with living cells
  </Step>

  <Step title="2010s: Application to Polymer Cross-Linking">
    Click chemistry adopted for polymer networks:
    - Orthogonal to radical polymerization
    - Precise control over cross-link density
    - **MINP synthesis**: Surface shell formation
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Azide-Alkyne Cycloaddition Mechanism

### Uncatalyzed Reaction (Huisgen 1,3-Dipolar Cycloaddition)

**Thermal reaction** at high temperature (100-150°C):

$$\text{R-N}_3 + \text{R'-C}\equiv\text{CH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{R-triazole-R'}$$

**Problems**:
- Requires high temperature (incompatible with MINPs)
- Mixture of 1,4- and 1,5-regioisomers
- Slow reaction (days)

---

### Copper(I)-Catalyzed AAC (CuAAC) - The Gold Standard

**Mechanism** (simplified):

<Steps>
  <Step title="Alkyne Activation">
    Cu(I) coordinates to terminal alkyne, increasing acidity of alkyne proton.

    $$\text{R-C}\equiv\text{CH} + \text{Cu(I)} \rightarrow \text{R-C}\equiv\text{C-Cu} + \text{H}^+$$

    **Result**: Copper acetylide intermediate
  </Step>

  <Step title="Azide Coordination">
    Azide nitrogen coordinates to copper:

    $$\text{R'-N}_3 + \text{Cu-acetylide} \rightarrow \text{Complex}$$

    **Result**: Activated azide-alkyne complex
  </Step>

  <Step title="Cycloaddition">
    Concerted [3+2] cycloaddition forms 5-membered triazole ring:

    **Result**: 1,4-disubstituted 1,2,3-triazole (single regioisomer)
  </Step>

  <Step title="Product Release">
    Triazole product dissociates, regenerating Cu(I) catalyst:

    $$\text{Cu(I)} + \text{triazole-product}$$

    **Result**: Catalyst available for next cycle
  </Step>
</Steps>

---

### CuAAC Reaction Conditions

**Optimal parameters for MINP synthesis**:

| Parameter | Value | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| **Catalyst** | CuSO₄ + sodium ascorbate | In situ reduction to Cu(I) |
| **Solvent** | Water, DMF, or mixed | Depends on monomer solubility |
| **Temperature** | 20-40°C | Room temperature works |
| **Time** | 4-24 hours | Faster with higher catalyst loading |
| **pH** | 7-9 | Slightly basic optimal |

**Catalyst loading**: 5-20 mol% Cu relative to azide/alkyne

<Warning>
  Cu(I) is air-sensitive and oxidizes to Cu(II) (inactive). Use oxygen-free conditions or add reducing agent (ascorbate).
</Warning>

## Click Chemistry in MINP Synthesis

### Why Click for Surface Cross-Linking?

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Orthogonality" icon="arrows-split-up-and-left">
    **Independent of radical polymerization**:
    - Azide and alkyne don't participate in radical reactions
    - Can perform click before, during, or after core polymerization
    - No interference between surface and core chemistry
  </Card>

  <Card title="Biocompatibility" icon="heart-pulse">
    **Suitable for biological applications**:
    - Azides and alkynes inert in biological systems
    - Aqueous conditions
    - Mild temperature
    - MINPs can be used in vivo
  </Card>

  <Card title="Modularity" icon="puzzle-piece">
    **Easy to vary cross-link density**:
    - Adjust azide:alkyne ratio
    - Control reaction time
    - Sequential clicking for multi-layer structures
  </Card>

  <Card title="Robustness" icon="shield">
    **Triazole product is stable**:
    - Resistant to hydrolysis
    - Stable to pH 2-12
    - Thermally stable (>200°C)
    - Permanent cross-link
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### MINP Surface Architecture

**Two-component system**:

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Monoazide Linkers">
    **Azide-bearing molecules** that partition to micelle surface.

    **Structure**:
    ```
    R-O-(CH₂)₃-N₃
    ```

    **Properties**:
    - Hydrophilic headgroup (R = PEG, carboxylate)
    - Azide at terminus for surface exposure
    - Water-soluble

    **Typical composition**: 10-20 molecules per MINP

    **After clicking**: Forms triazole cross-links to surfactants
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Alkyne Surfactants">
    **Surfactant molecules with terminal alkyne groups**.

    **Structure**:
    ```
    CH₂=C(CH₃)-COO-(CH₂)₁₁-C≡CH
    ```

    **Properties**:
    - Methacrylate head (polymerizable)
    - Long alkyl chain (hydrophobic)
    - Terminal alkyne (click handle)

    **Typical composition**: 30-40 molecules per MINP

    **Role**: Forms micelle shell, connects to core via polymerization
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

### Surface Cross-Linking Protocol

**Integration with MINP workflow** (Phase 2):

<Steps>
  <Step title="Micelle Assembly">
    Mix components in aqueous buffer:
    - Template (center)
    - Alkyne surfactants (shell)
    - Monoazide linkers (surface)
    - DVB cross-linkers (core)

    **Self-assembly** forms micelle structure (~20 nm)
  </Step>

  <Step title="Click Reaction">
    Add Cu(I) catalyst:
    ```bash
    # Typical reaction mixture
    - Micelle solution: 10 mL
    - CuSO₄: 5 mM
    - Sodium ascorbate: 10 mM
    - Stir at 25°C, 12 hours
    ```

    **Result**: Azide-alkyne pairs form triazole bonds at surface
  </Step>

  <Step title="Verification">
    LNKD prediction + experimental check:
    - **Computational**: LNKD predicts 100-150 surface pairs
    - **Experimental**: FTIR shows azide disappearance (2100 cm⁻¹)
    - **Experimental**: NMR shows triazole proton (δ 7-8 ppm)
  </Step>

  <Step title="Proceed to Core Polymerization">
    After surface click, initiate radical polymerization for core:
    - UV or thermal initiator
    - Forms C-C bonds between DVB monomers
    - Surface shell constrains core during polymerization
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Triazole Product Properties

### Chemical Structure

**1,4-Disubstituted 1,2,3-triazole**:

**Key features**:
- Aromatic (6 π electrons)
- Planar geometry
- H-bond acceptor (three N atoms)
- Weak H-bond donor (C-H at position 5)

---

### Physical Properties

| Property | Value | Relevance |
|----------|-------|-----------|
| **Bond length** | C-N: 1.35-1.40 Å | Typical for aromatic C-N |
| **Stability** | ΔHf = -273 kJ/mol | Very stable (high formation energy) |
| **Polarity** | Dipole: ~4-5 D | Moderate polarity |
| **pKa** | ~9-10 (N-H if present) | Weakly basic |

**Why triazoles are excellent cross-links**:
1. **Stable**: Won't hydrolyze or decompose
2. **Rigid**: Planar ring maintains MINP structure
3. **Polar**: Compatible with aqueous environments
4. **Biocompatible**: Triazole motif found in drugs

---

### Spectroscopic Signatures

<Accordion title="IR Spectroscopy">
  **Diagnostic peaks**:

  | Feature | Wavenumber (cm⁻¹) | Assignment |
  |---------|-------------------|------------|
  | Azide N₃ | 2100-2150 | Asymmetric stretch (starting material) |
  | Triazole C=N | 1500-1600 | Ring stretch (product) |
  | Triazole C-H | 3100-3150 | Aromatic C-H (product) |

  **Reaction monitoring**: Azide peak (2100 cm⁻¹) disappears, triazole peaks (1500-1600 cm⁻¹) appear

  **Conversion calculation**:
  $$\text{Conversion} = \frac{A_{2100, \, t=0} - A_{2100, \, t}}{A_{2100, \, t=0}} \times 100\%$$
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="NMR Spectroscopy">
  **¹H NMR diagnostic signals**:

  | Signal | δ (ppm) | Assignment |
  |--------|---------|------------|
  | Alkyne C-H | 1.9-2.5 | Terminal alkyne (starting material) |
  | Triazole C-H | 7.5-8.5 | Aromatic proton at position 5 (product) |
  | Azide CH₂ | 3.2-3.5 | Protons α to N₃ (shifts upon clicking) |

  **Example**:
  ```
  Before clicking: δ 2.3 (t, 1H, alkyne C-H), 3.3 (t, 2H, CH₂-N₃)
  After clicking:  δ 7.8 (s, 1H, triazole C-H), 3.5 (t, 2H, CH₂-triazole)
  ```
</Accordion>

## LNKD's Role in Click Cross-Linking

### Prediction Task

**Question**: Which azide-alkyne pairs will click during MINP synthesis?

**Challenge**:
- 30 alkyne surfactants × 20 monoazide linkers = 600 possible pairs
- Not all pairs geometrically accessible
- Some alkynes/azides buried, some exposed

**LNKD's solution**:
```bash
python3 predict_for_single_surfactant.py \
    micelle_equilibrated.pdb \
    dummy_core.txt \              # No core prediction yet
    sur_reactive.txt \            # Azide N and alkyne C atoms
    -sur_QR 15 \                  # Larger radius for surface
    -sur_W 0.03 \
    -o surface_output/
```

**Output**: 100-150 predicted azide-alkyne pairs (representing 50-75 click reactions, since each forms 2 bonds)

---

### Topology Update for MD

After LNKD prediction, update GROMACS topology to include triazole bonds:

**From `surface_pair_output.txt`**:
```
59   1054
4602 5222
```

**Add to `topol.top`**:
```
[ bonds ]
; ai  aj  funct  c0      c1
; Existing bonds...

; LNKD-predicted triazole bonds
59   1054   1   0.140   400000  ; N-C bond in triazole
4602 5222   1   0.140   400000  ; N-C bond in triazole
```

**Bond parameters**:
- Type 1: Harmonic potential
- Length: 0.140 nm (N-C in aromatic triazole)
- Force constant: 400,000 kJ/mol/nm²

---

### Constrained MD Simulation

Use PLUMED to enforce predicted bonds during MD equilibration:

```python
# plumed_surface.dat
DISTANCE ATOMS=59,1054 LABEL=d1
UPPER_WALLS ARG=d1 AT=0.35 KAPPA=1000.0 EXP=2

DISTANCE ATOMS=4602,5222 LABEL=d2
UPPER_WALLS ARG=d2 AT=0.35 KAPPA=1000.0 EXP=2

# ... all predicted pairs

PRINT ARG=d1,d2,... FILE=distances.dat STRIDE=100
```

**Run MD**:
```bash
gmx mdrun -v -deffnm surface_md -plumed plumed_surface.dat
```

**Result**: Structure with surface cross-links enforced, ready for core polymerization

    - Check for aggregation

    - Diameter: 15-25 nm
    - Spherical morphology
    - Dense shell, lighter core (before core polymerization)
  </Accordion>

## Advantages vs. Other Cross-Linking Methods

| Method | Advantages | Disadvantages | MINP Suitability |
|--------|-----------|---------------|------------------|
| **Click chemistry** | Orthogonal, biocompatible, high yield | Requires Cu (toxic) | **Excellent** (surface) |
| **Radical polymerization** | Simple, fast, cheap | Non-selective, oxygen-sensitive | **Excellent** (core) |
| **Condensation** | Reversible (dynamic) | Water byproduct, equilibrium-limited | Poor (incomplete) |
| **Diels-Alder** | Thermal reversibility | High temperature, slow | Moderate (testing) |

**MINP strategy**: Combine click (surface) + radical (core) for best of both worlds
