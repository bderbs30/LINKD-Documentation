## What is Radical Polymerization?

**Radical polymerization** is a chain-growth process where reactive radical species add to vinyl monomers, forming C-C bonds.

**Key features**:
- Fast reaction (minutes to hours)
- High molecular weight polymers
- Tolerates many functional groups
- Inexpensive initiators

**MINP application**: Core cross-linking via C-C bond formation between divinylbenzene (DVB) and methacrylate monomers.

---

## Historical Development

<Steps>
  <Step title="1930s: Discovery of Free Radical Polymerization">
    **Staudinger and Carothers** demonstrate vinyl polymerization:
    - Styrene → polystyrene
    - Methyl methacrylate → PMMA (Plexiglas)
    - Initiated by heat, light, or peroxides
  </Step>

  <Step title="1940s-1950s: Mechanism Elucidation">
    **Flory, Norrish, and others** establish radical chain mechanism:
    - Initiation: radical generation
    - Propagation: chain growth
    - Termination: radical recombination
    - Kinetic equations developed
  </Step>

  <Step title="1960s: Cross-Linking Polymers">
    **Introduction of divinyl monomers**:
    - DVB added to styrene → cross-linked polystyrene
    - EGDMA + MMA → cross-linked network
    - Basis for ion exchange resins, chromatography media
  </Step>

  <Step title="1990s-2000s: Controlled Radical Polymerization">
    **Living radical polymerization** techniques:
    - ATRP (Atom Transfer Radical Polymerization)
    - RAFT (Reversible Addition-Fragmentation chain Transfer)
    - NMP (Nitroxide-Mediated Polymerization)
    - Better control over molecular weight and architecture
  </Step>

  <Step title="2010s: Application to MINPs">
    **Radical cross-linking in nanoparticles**:
    - Microemulsion polymerization
    - Controlled cross-link density
    - **LNKD contribution**: Predict which vinyl carbons bond
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Free Radical Mechanism

### Three-Stage Process

Radical polymerization occurs in three distinct stages:

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="1. Initiation">
    **Generate reactive radicals** from stable initiator.

    **Thermal initiation** (AIBN):
    $$\text{AIBN} \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2 \text{R}^\bullet + \text{N}_2$$

    **UV initiation** (photoinitiator):
    $$\text{Photoinitiator} \xrightarrow{h\nu} 2 \text{R}^\bullet$$

    **Result**: Primary radicals (R•) that can attack vinyl groups

    **Rate**: Slow (rate-determining step for initiation)
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="2. Propagation">
    **Chain growth** via radical addition to vinyl double bonds.

    **First addition**:
    $$\text{R}^\bullet + \text{CH}_2\text{=CH-R'} \rightarrow \text{R-CH}_2\text{-}\dot{\text{C}}\text{H-R'}$$

    **Subsequent additions** (repeat):
    $$\text{R-}(\text{CH}_2\text{-CHR'})_n\text{-}\dot{\text{C}}\text{H-R'} + \text{CH}_2\text{=CH-R'} \rightarrow \text{R-}(\text{CH}_2\text{-CHR'})_{n+1}\text{-}\dot{\text{C}}\text{H-R'}$$

    **Result**: Growing polymer chain with radical at terminus

    **Rate**: Fast (1000-10,000 additions per second)
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="3. Termination">
    **Radical destruction** via recombination or disproportionation.

    **Combination** (C-C bond formation):
    $$\text{R-}(\text{CH}_2\text{-CHR'})_n^\bullet + ^\bullet(\text{R'HC-CH}_2)_m\text{-R} \rightarrow \text{R-}(\text{CH}_2\text{-CHR'})_{n+m}\text{-R}$$

    **Disproportionation** (H-transfer):
    $$\text{R-}(\text{CH}_2\text{-CHR'})_n^\bullet + ^\bullet(\text{R'HC-CH}_2)_m\text{-R} \rightarrow \text{R-}(\text{CH}_2\text{-CHR'})_n\text{-H} + \text{R-}(\text{R'C=CH})(\text{CH}_2\text{-CHR'})_{m-1}\text{-R}$$

    **Result**: Terminated chains (no more radical)

    **Rate**: Very fast (diffusion-controlled)
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Vinyl Monomers for MINP Core

### Divinylbenzene (DVB) - Primary Cross-Linker

**Structure**:
```
        CH₂=CH-[benzene]-CH=CH₂
```

**Properties**:
- Two vinyl groups (difunctional)
- Aromatic core provides rigidity
- Hydrophobic
- Commercial DVB is mixture of ortho-, meta-, para-isomers

**Role in MINP**:
- Each DVB can form 2 C-C bonds → cross-linking
- Creates rigid core network
- Typical concentration: 60-80% of core monomers

**Reactivity**: Both vinyl groups react at similar rates (no preference)

---

### Methacrylate Monomers - Functional Cross-Linkers

**Ethylene Glycol Dimethacrylate (EGDMA)**:
```
CH₂=C(CH₃)-COO-CH₂-CH₂-OOC-C(CH₃)=CH₂
```

**Properties**:
- Two methacrylate groups (difunctional)
- Flexible spacer (ethylene glycol)
- More polar than DVB
- Often mixed with DVB for balanced rigidity/flexibility

**Monovinyl methacrylates** (if surfactant participates):
```
CH₂=C(CH₃)-COO-R
```
- Single vinyl group
- Can incorporate into core network
- Provides functional handles (R = various groups)

---

### Vinyl Carbon Reactivity

**Which carbons form radicals?**

<Accordion title="Terminal Vinyl Carbons (CH₂=)">
  **Less substituted carbon** in vinyl group.

  **Radical formation**:
  $$\text{CH}_2\text{=CH-R} + \text{R'}^\bullet \rightarrow \text{R'-CH}_2\text{-}\dot{\text{C}}\text{H-R}$$

  **Reason**: Less steric hindrance, more stable radical (α to substituent)

  **In LNKD**: Denoted as **CU** (Carbon Unsubstituted)
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Substituted Vinyl Carbons (=CH-)">
  **More substituted carbon** in vinyl group.

  **Radical formation**:
  $$\text{CH}_2\text{=CH-R} + \text{R'}^\bullet \rightarrow \text{R'-}\dot{\text{C}}\text{H-CH}_2\text{-R}$$

  **Reason**: Possible if radical attacks from other direction (less common)

  **In LNKD**: Denoted as **CV** (Carbon Vinyl position)
</Accordion>

**Selectivity**: In practice, radicals preferentially attack CH₂= position (~90%)

    - Initiators cost pennies per synthesis
    - Scalable to large batches
  </Card>

### Two-Step Cross-Linking Strategy

**Why not radical polymerization for everything?**

| Aspect | Surface (Click) | Core (Radical) |
|--------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Selectivity** | High (azide + alkyne only) | Low (any vinyl group) |
| **Control** | Precise pair prediction | Random cross-linking |
| **Orthogonality** | Doesn't interfere with radicals | Consumes all vinyl groups |
| **Order** | **First** (defines shell) | **Second** (fills core) |

**Strategy**:
1. **Click chemistry**: Create rigid surface shell
2. **Radical polymerization**: Fill and cross-link core

<Warning>
  If radical polymerization is performed first, it will consume both surface and core vinyl groups, preventing click chemistry.
</Warning>

## LNKD Prediction for Core Cross-Linking

### Challenge: Unpaired Radicals

**Problem**: Not all vinyl carbons find bonding partners.

**Example**:
- DVB molecule has 2 vinyl groups (4 vinyl carbons total: 2×CU, 2×CV)
- If only 1 vinyl group reacts, the other vinyl group is unreacted
- Unreacted vinyl = **radical** (reactive, unpaired electron)

**Consequence**: Radicals are chemically unstable and must be saturated with hydrogen.

---

**Input**: Surface-cross-linked structure + core reactive atoms

    -core_W 0.03 \
    empty_surface.txt \          # Surface already done

**Reactive atoms file** (`core_reactive.txt`):
```text

CV  DVO    # Vinyl carbon (vinyl position) on DVB ortho-isomer
CU  DVP    # Vinyl carbon on DVB para-isomer

```

**Output files**:

   ...
   ```

2. **`radical_output.txt`**: Unpaired vinyl carbons

   ```
   **Interpretation**: These atoms didn't find partners → need H-saturation

## Hydrogen Saturation of Radicals

### Why H-Saturation is Required

**Problem**: Unpaired radicals are reactive
- Can undergo unwanted reactions
- Destabilize MD simulations
- Not chemically realistic (would abstract H from surroundings in reality)

**Solution**: Add hydrogen atoms to radical centers

$$\dot{\text{C}}\text{H-R} + \text{H}^\bullet \rightarrow \text{CH}_2\text{-R}$$

---

### H-Saturation Protocol

**Computational approach**:

```python
# h_saturate.py
import MDAnalysis as mda

# Load structure and radical list
u = mda.Universe('surface_md.gro')
with open('core_output/radical_output.txt') as f:
    radicals = [int(line.strip()) for line in f if line.strip().isdigit()]

for radical_idx in radicals:
    atom = u.atoms[radical_idx - 1]  # Convert to 0-indexing

    # Determine geometry for H addition
    if atom.name in ['CU', 'CV']:
        # Vinyl carbon: add H to complete sp³ hybridization
        add_hydrogen_to_vinyl(atom, u)

# Write saturated structure
u.atoms.write('core_saturated.pdb')

def add_hydrogen_to_vinyl(carbon_atom, universe):
    """
    Add hydrogen to vinyl carbon to satisfy valence.

    Geometry: tetrahedral around carbon (sp³ after H addition)
    """
    # Get carbon's existing bonds
    bonds = carbon_atom.bonds

    # Calculate ideal H position (opposite to existing bonds)
    h_position = calculate_tetrahedral_position(carbon_atom, bonds)

    # Add hydrogen atom
    new_h = add_atom(universe, name='H', position=h_position)

    # Add bond in topology
    carbon_atom.add_bond(new_h)
```

**Validation**: Check that all radical atoms now have complete valence (4 bonds for C)

## Constrained MD for Core Cross-Linking

### PLUMED Restraints

Similar to surface, enforce predicted C-C bonds:

```python
# plumed_core.dat
# Core C-C bond restraints

DISTANCE ATOMS=137,249 LABEL=d1
UPPER_WALLS ARG=d1 AT=0.35 KAPPA=1000.0 EXP=2 LABEL=uw1

DISTANCE ATOMS=401,587 LABEL=d2
UPPER_WALLS ARG=d2 AT=0.35 KAPPA=1000.0 EXP=2 LABEL=uw2

# ... all core pairs

PRINT ARG=d1,d2,... FILE=core_distances.dat STRIDE=100
```

**Parameters**:
- `AT=0.35`: Upper wall at 3.5 Å (allows flexibility)
- `KAPPA=1000`: Force constant (kJ/mol/nm²)
- `EXP=2`: Harmonic restraint

---

### Run Constrained MD

```bash
# Prepare simulation
gmx grompp -f md_core.mdp \
           -c core_saturated.gro \
           -p topol.top \
           -o core_md.tpr

# Run with PLUMED
gmx mdrun -v -deffnm core_md -plumed plumed_core.dat
```

**Duration**: 5-10 ns (sufficient for bond formation)

**Monitor**:
```bash
# Check distances converge
tail core_distances.dat

# All distances should be < 3.5 Å and stable
```

**Output**: `core_md.gro` - Fully cross-linked MINP structure

## Experimental Core Cross-Linking

### Radical Polymerization Conditions

**Typical MINP core polymerization**:

| Parameter | Value | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| **Initiator** | AIBN or UV photoinitiator | 1-2 mol% |
| **Temperature** | 60-80°C (AIBN) or RT (UV) | Depends on initiator |
| **Time** | 4-12 hours | Until vinyl peaks disappear |
| **Atmosphere** | Nitrogen or argon | Oxygen inhibits radicals |
| **Solvent** | Toluene, chloroform, or aqueous | Depends on MINP type |

**Monitoring**:
- **FTIR**: Vinyl C=C stretch at 1640 cm⁻¹ disappears
- **NMR**: Vinyl protons (δ 5-6 ppm) disappear

---

### Polymerization Kinetics

**Conversion vs. time** (typical):

| Time | Vinyl Conversion | Structure |
|------|------------------|-----------|
| 0 h | 0% | Pre-polymerization |
| 2 h | 40-60% | Gelation (network forms) |
| 4 h | 70-85% | Dense core |
| 8 h | 85-95% | Nearly complete |
| 12 h | 90-98% | Final structure |

**Gelation point**: When cross-linked network spans entire particle (~50-60% conversion)

<Accordion title="What is Gelation?">
  **Gelation**: Transition from liquid-like (sol) to solid-like (gel) state.

  **Cause**: Cross-linking creates infinite network

  **Observation**:
  - Solution viscosity increases dramatically
  - Particles no longer flow freely
  - Network is permanent (cannot re-dissolve)

  **Relevance**: After gelation, MINP structure is locked in place
</Accordion>

## Oxygen Inhibition

### The Oxygen Problem

**Oxygen is a radical scavenger**:

$$\text{R}^\bullet + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{R-O-O}^\bullet$$

**Peroxy radical (R-O-O•)** is much less reactive:
- Does not propagate polymerization
- Terminates growing chains
- Requires ~100× more initiator to overcome

**Result**: Polymerization slows or stops in presence of air

---

### Degassing Methods

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Freeze-Pump-Thaw">
    **Gold standard for small-scale synthesis**:

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Freeze">
        Freeze solution in liquid nitrogen
      </Step>
      <Step title="Evacuate">
        Apply vacuum to remove gases (including O₂)
      </Step>
      <Step title="Thaw">
        Warm to room temperature
      </Step>
      <Step title="Repeat">
        Cycle 3-5 times for complete degassing
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    **Effectiveness**: >99.9% O₂ removal
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Nitrogen/Argon Sparging">
    **Simple method for larger volumes**:

    - Bubble inert gas through solution for 15-30 min
    - Displaces dissolved oxygen
    - Maintain inert atmosphere during polymerization

    **Effectiveness**: ~95-98% O₂ removal (sufficient for most applications)
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Chemical Scavenging">
    **Add reducing agent**:

    - Ascorbic acid
    - Glucose + glucose oxidase
    - Consumes residual O₂ chemically

    **Effectiveness**: Good for aqueous systems where sparging is difficult
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Comparison: Click vs. Radical

### When to Use Each Method

| Criterion | Click Chemistry | Radical Polymerization |
|-----------|----------------|------------------------|
| **Selectivity** | Azide + alkyne only | Any vinyl group |
| **Speed** | 8-24 hours | 2-8 hours |
| **Temperature** | 20-40°C | 60-80°C (or RT with UV) |
| **Oxygen sensitivity** | Insensitive | **Highly sensitive** |
| **Catalyst** | Cu(I) required | Initiator only |
| **Product** | Triazole (aromatic) | C-C bond (aliphatic) |
| **Reversibility** | Irreversible | Irreversible |
| **Water compatibility** | Excellent | Good (with water-soluble initiators) |

**MINP strategy**: Click for surface (control), radical for core (speed and density)

## LNKD's Impact on Core Cross-Linking

### Before LNKD

**Challenges**:
- No prediction of which vinyl groups react
- Radical distribution unknown
- Trial-and-error cross-linker ratios
- Iterative MD simulations (weeks per attempt)

**Result**: Sub-optimal core structures, weak mechanical properties

---

### With LNKD

**Advantages**:
1. **Predicts bonding pairs**: Know which vinyl carbons form C-C bonds
2. **Identifies radicals**: Target H-saturation precisely
3. **Fast computation**: Direct topology prediction vs. iterative MD
4. **Rational design**: Optimize DVB:EGDMA ratio computationally

**Result**: Stronger, more uniform MINP cores
